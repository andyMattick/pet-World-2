## The Transatlantic Slave Trade (6.10)  (15 multiple choice + 3 newer-type)

*Lesson (shown when she misses it):* From the 1500s to the 1860s, about 12.5 million Africans were forced onto ships and taken across the Atlantic, mostly to work on sugar plantations in Brazil and the Caribbean. It tore apart families and communities in Africa, and enslaved people resisted in many ways.

1. L1: Between about 1500 and the 1860s, about how many African people were forced onto ships in the transatlantic slave trade?
   - **About 12.5 million**
   - About 125,000
   - About 1 million
   - More than 100 million
2. L1: What do historians call the voyage across the Atlantic on slave ships?
   - **The Middle Passage**
   - The Silk Road
   - The Northwest Passage
   - The Grand Tour
3. L2: Where were most enslaved Africans taken?
   - **Brazil and the Caribbean**
   - The colonies that became the United States
   - Western Europe
   - The Ottoman Empire
4. L2: Which crop drove much of the demand for enslaved workers in Brazil and the Caribbean?
   - **Sugar**
   - Wheat
   - Potatoes
   - Tea
5. L2: Slavery existed in many societies before 1500. Which feature made Atlantic slavery different?
   - **It was based on race, and children were born into slavery**
   - Enslaved people in the Americas were paid fair wages for work
   - It lasted a few years for each person, then ended
   - It happened in one small region of Africa
6. L2: Captives were chained below deck in crowded ships for weeks or months. Of the 12.5 million people forced aboard, about how many died during the crossing?
   - **Nearly 2 million people**
   - About 2,000 people
   - About 200,000 people
   - About 20 million people
7. L3: In 1526, Afonso I, king of Kongo, wrote to the king of Portugal (adapted): “Merchants are taking our people every day, even our own relatives and nobles. We ask you to stop it.” What does this source show?
   - **An African ruler protested the trade and its harm**
   - Kongo’s king asked for more ships to come
   - The trade had little effect on Kongo
   - Afonso wrote to thank the king of Portugal for the trade
8. L3: Why is Afonso’s letter especially useful to historians?
   - **It shows an African leader’s view at the time**
   - It was written by a ship captain
   - It was written centuries later
   - It lists the names of the people who were taken away
9. L2: Domingos Álvares was a healer from the kingdom of Dahomey in West Africa. What happened to him?
   - **He was enslaved and taken to Brazil, where he kept healing**
   - He sailed to Portugal as an ambassador from Dahomey
   - He became king of Dahomey and ended the trade in his lands
   - He was a sailor who traded in the Indian Ocean
10. L3: Why do historians tell the life stories of people like Domingos Álvares?
   - **They show enslaved people as individuals with skills and beliefs**
   - They prove the trade was much smaller than historians once thought
   - They are easier to find than ship records
   - They show how kings in Europe lived
11. L3: West and Central Africa lost millions of people, most of them young adults. Which effect does this help explain?
   - **Fewer young people to farm, build, and raise families**
   - Faster population growth in Africa than anywhere in Europe
   - More African sailors trading in the Indian Ocean
   - Stronger ties between Kongo and the Inca
12. L2: Some enslaved people escaped and built their own free communities, like Palmares in Brazil. What are such communities called?
   - **Maroon communities**
   - Missions
   - Encomienda estates
   - Chinampas
13. L3: In his book Capitalism and Slavery (1944), the historian Eric Williams argued that profits from slavery helped Britain grow rich. What kind of claim is this?
   - **An argument about causes that historians still discuss**
   - A primary source written by a ship captain in the 1600s
   - A fact that no historian has ever questioned
   - A description of the Middle Passage
14. L2: Cape Coast Castle in Ghana is now a museum. What happened there during the slave trade?
   - **Captives were held there before being forced onto ships**
   - It was a palace for the Ottoman sultans
   - It was a school where Portuguese sailors learned to navigate
   - Sugar was grown and sold there
15. L3: Why do many Ghanaians and people of African descent visit castles like Cape Coast today?
   - **To remember the people who suffered and honor their ancestors**
   - To see where Portuguese explorers first landed on their way to Asia
   - To study how sugar was grown in the Caribbean
   - To learn about Mongol trade routes in Africa

Newer-type questions:

- Put in order (L2): Put these events in order. **Portuguese ships reach West Africa (1440s) → Afonso I of Kongo writes to Portugal’s king (1526) → Sugar plantations spread in Brazil (late 1500s) → Domingos Álvares is taken to Brazil (about 1730)**
- Select all (L2): Select every way enslaved Africans and their descendants resisted.
   - **Revolts on ships and plantations**
   - **Escaping to form maroon communities like Palmares**
   - **Keeping their languages, music, and beliefs alive**
   - Signing the Treaty of Tordesillas
   - Paying the encomienda tax
- Short answer (15+ words): Explain one cause and one effect of the transatlantic slave trade.
   - Rubric: Gives an accurate cause (demand for workers on sugar plantations, the deaths of Indigenous workers from disease, profits for European traders and planters); Gives an accurate effect (millions of Africans taken from their homes, the loss of people in West and Central Africa, wealth for colonies and traders, African cultures carried to the Americas)
   - Model: *One cause was the demand for workers on sugar plantations in Brazil and the Caribbean, after disease had killed many Indigenous people. One effect was that about 12.5 million Africans were forced onto ships, and West and Central Africa lost millions of people.*

## The Silk Roads and the Mongol Empire (6.2)  (15 multiple choice + 3 newer-type)

*Lesson (shown when she misses it):* Temüjin (Genghis Khan) united the Mongols in 1206, and his horse archers built the largest land empire in history. Relay stations (the yam), travel passes, and tolerance for many faiths kept the Silk Roads busy under the Pax Mongolica.

   - A school where Mongol princes learned to read
3. L2: A merchant shows you a paiza, a metal tablet from the khan. Why does it help him?

   - It is a map of the roads to Karakorum and beyond

   - Merchants switched from camels to wagons pulled by oxen
   - The Mongols closed the sea routes, so land routes were busier
5. L2: Historians call the long stretch of order across Mongol lands the Pax Mongolica. What does it mean?

   - The Mongol road
   - The Mongol army code

   - They forced conquered peoples to follow one Mongol faith

   - The khan made Christianity the law of the empire
   - Karakorum was a small village with few visitors
   - Religious leaders were banned from the khan’s court
8. L3: Rubruck was a Christian friar who hoped to convert the Mongols. How might that shape his account?
   - **He may pay close attention to how Christians were treated**
   - He could not have understood anything he saw at court
   - He was writing to describe life in Ming China
   - He probably copied his whole report from an older Roman book
9. L2: Sorqoqtani Beki was the mother of Kublai Khan and three other rulers. What is she remembered for?

   - Leading the Mongol fleets that attacked Japan in 1274 and 1281
   - Building the Great Wall to keep the Mongols out
10. L2: Rashid al-Din, a Persian official who served the Mongol rulers of Iran, wrote a giant book. What was it?

   - A collection of laws for the yam riders
   - A book of Mongol war songs
11. L1: Which grandson of Genghis Khan conquered China and founded the Yuan dynasty?
   - **Kublai Khan**
   - Batu Khan
   - Hulagu Khan
   - Temüjin
12. L3: Which evidence best supports the claim that the Mongol Empire connected Afro-Eurasia?
   - **Marco Polo traveled from Venice to Kublai Khan’s court**
   - The Mongols lived in felt tents called gers
   - Genghis Khan united the Mongol tribes in 1206
   - Mongol soldiers were skilled horse archers who rode for days
13. L3: A visitor asks why the Mongol armies were so hard to stop. What is the best answer?

   - They had a navy larger than any other in Afro-Eurasia
   - Their enemies had no armies or city walls

14. L2: After about 1260, the empire split into four parts called khanates. Which was one of them?
   - **The Golden Horde**
   - The Ottoman Empire

   - The Mongols lived in felt tents called gers.

## The Mongol Empire and The Black Death (6.3)  (14 multiple choice + 3 newer-type)

*Lesson (shown when she misses it):* The Black Death was a plague caused by bacteria spread by fleas on rats and by very sick people. It traveled along trade routes across Mongol lands and by ship from Kaffa, killing a third or more of Europe’s people from 1347 to 1351.

   - Bad air rising from swamps and old graves

3. L2: Genoese ships carried the plague to Italy in 1347 from which Black Sea trading port?

   - Lisbon, in Portugal
   - Alexandria, in Egypt
4. L2: Why did the plague spread so far so fast?
   - **Busy trade routes carried people, goods, and fleas**
   - Doctors moved from town to town to spread their cures
   - Cold winters made the fleas multiply faster
   - Farmers moved into cities when their crops failed
5. L1: About how much of Europe’s population died in the Black Death?

   - About one tenth
   - About nine out of ten

8. L3: Boccaccio wrote this a few years after the plague, at the start of a book of stories. How should a historian use his description?

   - Use it to learn about the Mongol army
9. L2: So many workers died that there were not enough left to farm. What did this mean for the peasants who survived?

   - The Protestant Reformation of 1517 in Germany
11. L3: In some towns, Christians falsely blamed their Jewish neighbors for the plague and attacked them. What does this show?

   - Doctors had found the real cause of the plague
   - The plague was over by the time the attacks began
   - Towns worked together to care for the sick and the poor
12. L2: Scientists have studied DNA from skeletons in plague graves. Why is this useful?

   - It proves the plague did not reach China
   - It shows the victims were mostly soldiers
13. L3: Which statement best links the Mongol Empire and the Black Death?
   - **Trade across Mongol lands helped carry the plague west**
   - The Mongols ended the plague by closing their borders
   - The plague began in Europe and spread east to Mongolia

14. L3: Gabriele de’ Mussis wrote that Mongol soldiers attacking Kaffa threw plague-infected bodies over the walls. He was not there himself. How should historians treat this story?
