## Introduction to adjectives and articles

Quiz: Adjectives and articles Quiz

### Meet the adjective

Lesson: An adjective describes a noun or pronoun. It tells what kind (a muddy boot), how many (three boots), or which one (the left boot). It can come before the noun or after a linking verb: the soup tastes salty.

1. (L1) Which word is an adjective? The tired puppy slept on the rug.
   - **tired**
   - puppy
   - slept
   - rug
2. (L1) Which word is an adjective? We climbed a steep hill.
   - **steep**
   - climbed
   - hill
   - We
3. (L1) Which word describes the noun “jacket”? Mom bought a waterproof jacket.
   - **waterproof**
   - bought
   - Mom
   - jacket
4. (L1) Which word could describe a noun?
   - **noisy**
   - noise
   - quickly
   - under
5. (L2) What question does the adjective answer? Three goats crossed the bridge.
   - **How many?**
   - What kind?
   - Which one?
   - Where?
6. (L2) Which word is an adjective? The soup in the bowl smells delicious.
   - **delicious**
   - smells
   - soup
   - bowl
7. (L2) Not counting a, an, and the, how many adjectives are in this sentence? A small brown bird sang a cheerful song.
   - **3**
   - 2
   - 4
   - 1
8. (L2) In which sentence is “light” an adjective?
   - **She packed a light suitcase.**
   - Please light the candle.
   - The light flickered all night.

9. (L3) Which word does the adjective “brave” describe? The firefighter was brave during the storm.
   - **firefighter**

12. (L3) Why does “Spanish” start with a capital letter in “We ate Spanish rice”?

  - Rubric: Writes a complete sentence with at least two adjectives / Names the noun each adjective describes, correctly
  - Model: I love warm, cheesy pizza. “Warm” and “cheesy” both describe the noun “pizza.”

### Meet the article

Lesson: A, an, and the are articles, a small kind of adjective that comes before a noun (sometimes with other adjectives in between): the tall tree, an old map.

   - a, and, the
   - an, on, the
   - the, this, that

   - **an**

   - coin

Newer types:

- Tap the words (L1): Tap every article. **An** eagle landed on **the** branch of **a** tall pine.
- Select all (L2): Select all the articles. **a** · **the** · **an** · this · my · and
- Short answer (at least 15 words): Explain the difference between “a dog” and “the dog.” Use each one in a sentence.
  - Rubric: Says “a dog” means any dog, not a particular one / Says “the dog” means one particular dog the reader knows about / Uses each one correctly in a sentence
  - Model: “A dog” could be any dog: “I want a dog someday.” “The dog” is one particular dog: “The dog next door barks at night.”

### Choosing between definite and indefinite articles

Lesson: The points to one particular thing the reader knows about. A and an mean any one. Use an before a vowel sound (an hour, an umbrella) and a before a consonant sound (a unicorn, a one-way street).

## Introduction to adverbs

Quiz: Adverbs Quiz

### Meet the adverb

Lesson: An adverb describes a verb, an adjective, or another adverb. It tells how (slowly), when (tomorrow), where (outside), how often (always), or how much (very). Many end in -ly, but not all: soon, fast, well.

1. (L1) Which word is the adverb? The turtle moved slowly across the yard.
   - **slowly**
   - turtle
   - moved
   - across
2. (L1) Which word is the adverb? We will leave tomorrow.
   - **tomorrow**
   - leave
   - will
   - We
3. (L1) Which word tells where? The kids played games outside.
   - **outside**
   - played
   - kids
   - games
4. (L1) Which word is an adverb?
   - **gently**
   - gentle
   - gentleness
   - gentlest
5. (L2) What question does the adverb answer? She always brushes her teeth.
   - **How often?**
   - Where?
   - What kind?
   - How many?
6. (L2) Which word does “carefully” describe? Leo carefully carried the tray.
   - **carried**
   - Leo
   - tray
   - the
7. (L2) Which word is the adverb? The test was very hard.
   - **very**
   - test
   - hard
   - was
8. (L2) How many adverbs are in this sentence? Yesterday, the dog barked loudly outside.
   - **3**
   - 2
   - 1
   - 4
9. (L3) In “The singer sang extremely well,” what does “extremely” describe?
   - **the adverb “well”**
   - the verb “sang”
   - the noun “singer”
   - the whole sentence at once
10. (L3) Which sentence uses “fast” as an adverb?
   - **Jamal ran fast.**
   - Jamal is a fast runner.
   - The fast car zoomed by.

11. (L3) Which word is an adverb even though it doesn’t end in -ly?

12. (L3) Which word is NOT an adverb?

13. (L3) Which sentence uses “well” as an adverb?
   - **Marta plays the violin well.**

   - The old well behind the barn was very deep.

14. (L3) Which word in this sentence is an adverb? Surprisingly, nobody noticed the huge mistake.
   - **Surprisingly**
   - nobody
   - noticed
   - huge

- Tap the words (L1): Tap every adverb. **Yesterday** the puppy barked **loudly** **outside.**

- Short answer (at least 12 words): Write a sentence with one adverb that tells how and another that tells when. Label each one.
  - Rubric: Writes a complete sentence / Uses an adverb that tells how and labels it / Uses an adverb that tells when and labels it

### Identifying relative adverbs

Lesson: Where, when, and why are relative adverbs when they start a clause that describes a noun: the park where we met, the day when it snowed, the reason why she laughed. When they ask a question, they are not relative adverbs.

1. (L1) Which word is a relative adverb? This is the park where we met.
   - **where**
   - This
   - park
   - met
2. (L1) Which relative adverb fits? I remember the day ___ we got our puppy.
   - **when**
   - where
   - why
   - what
3. (L1) Which relative adverb fits? That’s the store ___ Dad buys fresh bread.
   - **where**
   - when
   - why
   - who
4. (L1) Which words can be relative adverbs?
   - **where, when, why**
   - who, what, which
   - here, there, now
   - and, but, or
5. (L2) Which relative adverb fits? Tell me the reason ___ you were late.
   - **why**
   - where
   - when
   - how
6. (L2) In which sentence is “where” a relative adverb?
   - **We visited the town where my mom grew up.**
   - Where did you put my shoes?
   - Where is the nearest library in this town?
   - Where are we going for dinner?
7. (L2) In which sentence is “when” a relative adverb?
   - **Summer is the season when we swim every day.**
   - When does the movie start?
   - When will we get to Grandma’s house tonight?
   - When is your birthday?
8. (L2) Which sentence combines these correctly? “This is the lake. We caught three fish there.”
   - **This is the lake where we caught three fish.**
   - This is the lake when we caught three fish.
   - This is the lake why we caught three fish.
   - This is where the lake we caught three fish.
9. (L3) What does the relative clause describe? I found the box where Grandma kept her letters.
   - **the noun “box”**
   - the verb “found”
   - the noun “letters”
   - the pronoun “I”
10. (L3) In “Do you know the place where the trail ends?”, what job does “where” do?
   - **It starts a clause that describes the noun “place”**
   - It asks a question all by itself, like “Where is it?”
   - It names a place on the map
   - It describes the verb “ends”
11. (L3) Which sentence uses a relative adverb correctly?
   - **Nobody knows the reason why the lights went out.**
   - Nobody knows the reason where the lights went out.
   - Nobody knows the reason when the lights went out.
   - Nobody knows the time why the lights went out.
12. (L3) Which sentence has a relative adverb?
   - **That was the year when I learned to swim.**
   - When I learned to swim, I was only six years old.
   - I learned to swim at camp.
   - Did you learn to swim at camp?
13. (L3) What is “why” doing in “Why did the bell ring early?”
   - **It asks a question**
   - It joins a clause to the noun “reason”
   - It describes a noun in the sentence
   - It names a time
14. (L3) Which relative adverb fits? Saturday is the day ___ the library opens late.
   - **when**
   - where
   - why
   - which

- Tap the words (L2): Tap the relative adverb. I still remember the summer **when** we built a treehouse.
- Sort (L2): Which relative adverb fits? the kitchen ___ we bake → **where**; the morning ___ it snowed → **when**; the reason ___ she laughed → **why**; the beach ___ we found shells → **where**; the year ___ I turned ten → **when**; the reason ___ the game was canceled → **why**

  - Model: That is the field where we play soccer. The relative adverb is “where.”

### Commas and adjectives

Lesson: Put a comma between two adjectives only if you could switch them or join them with “and”: a cold, rainy day (a rainy and cold day). No comma when you can’t: a big red barn, a heavy winter coat.

1. (L1) Which sentence is punctuated correctly?
   - **It was a cold, rainy morning.**
   - It was a cold rainy, morning.
   - It was a, cold rainy morning.
   - It was a cold, rainy, morning.
2. (L1) Which sentence is punctuated correctly?
   - **She owns three gray cats.**
   - She owns three, gray cats.
   - She owns three gray, cats.
   - She owns, three gray cats.
3. (L1) Which phrase is punctuated correctly?
   - **a happy, excited crowd**
   - a happy excited, crowd
   - a, happy excited crowd
   - a happy, excited, crowd
4. (L1) Which phrase is punctuated correctly?
   - **a soft, fluffy blanket**
   - a soft fluffy, blanket
   - a soft, fluffy, blanket
   - a, soft fluffy blanket
5. (L2) Which test tells you to put a comma between two adjectives?
   - **You can switch their order and put “and” between them**
   - Both adjectives are long words with three or more syllables
   - The adjectives come after the noun they describe
   - One of the adjectives ends in -y
6. (L2) Which phrase needs a comma?
   - **a friendly helpful neighbor**
   - a small brown dog next door
   - three red apples
   - a big wooden box
7. (L2) Which sentence is punctuated correctly?
   - **We watched a long, boring movie.**
   - We watched a long boring, movie.
   - We watched, a long boring movie.
   - We watched a long, boring, movie.
8. (L2) Where does the comma go in “a bright cheerful smile”?
   - **between “bright” and “cheerful”**
   - after “a”
   - between “cheerful” and “smile”
   - nowhere, because no comma is needed in this phrase
9. (L3) Why is there no comma in “a big wooden box”?
   - **“A wooden big box” and “a big and wooden box” both sound wrong**
   - Both adjectives are short words
   - “Wooden” is really a noun in this phrase
   - Commas never go between two adjectives that come before a noun
10. (L3) Which sentence uses commas correctly?
   - **The tired, hungry hikers reached the old stone cabin.**
   - The tired hungry hikers reached the old, stone cabin.
   - The tired, hungry hikers reached the old, stone cabin.
   - The tired hungry, hikers reached the old stone cabin.
11. (L3) Which sentence needs a comma between two adjectives?
   - **It was a dark stormy night.**

   - She wore a red wool hat.

   - **No comma: “winter coat” is one idea, so the words can’t switch**
   - A comma, because there are two adjectives before the noun “coat”

  - Rubric: Explains the switch test or the “and” test / Gives a correct example with a comma / Gives a correct example without a comma
  - Model: If the adjectives can switch places or be joined with “and,” use a comma: “a cold, rainy day.” If they can’t, don’t: “a big red barn.”

## Comparative, superlative, intensifiers, and adverbs of degree

Quiz: Comparing and intensifying Quiz

### Comparative and superlative adjectives and adverbs

Lesson: Compare two with -er or more (taller, more careful). Compare three or more with -est or most (tallest, most careful). Some are irregular: good, better, best; bad, worse, worst. Never use both: not “more taller.”

1. (L1) Which word fits? My dog is ___ than your dog.
   - **smaller**
   - small
   - smallest
   - more small
2. (L1) Which word fits? Of all the planets, Jupiter is the ___.
   - **largest**
   - larger
   - most large
   - large
3. (L1) Which word compares two things?
   - **taller**
   - tall
   - tallest
   - most tall
4. (L1) Which sentence uses a superlative?

   - Today is hotter than yesterday and the day before.

   - Today is hot and sunny.

   - This test was easiest than the last one.
   - This test was more easy than the last one.

7. (L2) Which sentence is correct?

   - My cold is worst today than yesterday.
   - My cold is more bad today than yesterday.
8. (L2) Which sentence is correct?

   - Of the three runners, Kai ran more fast.
9. (L3) Which sentence compares two actions correctly?

- Short answer (at least 15 words): Explain when to add -er and when to use “more.” Give one example of each.
  - Rubric: Says short words (one syllable, and some two-syllable words like happy) usually take -er / Says longer words and most -ly adverbs take “more” / Gives a correct example of each

### Intensifiers and adverbs of degree

Lesson: Adverbs of degree tell how much: very, extremely, quite, almost, barely, too. They make an adjective or adverb stronger (extremely cold) or weaker (slightly cold). Too means more than is good: too hot to drink.

1. (L1) Which word is an adverb of degree? The soup is very hot.
   - **very**
   - soup
   - hot
   - is
2. (L1) Which word tells how much? I am almost finished.
   - **almost**
   - I
   - am
   - finished
3. (L1) Which word makes “tired” stronger? I was ___ tired after the race.
   - **extremely**
   - slightly
   - somewhat
   - a little
4. (L1) Which sentence uses an intensifier?
   - **The water was incredibly cold.**
   - The water was cold.
   - The cold water splashed.
   - We swam in the cold water of the lake.
5. (L2) Which word makes the adjective weaker? The movie was ___ funny.
   - **somewhat**
   - very
   - really
   - incredibly
6. (L2) Which sentence means the box is more than you can lift?
   - **The box is too heavy for me to lift.**
   - The box is very heavy, but I can lift it.
   - The box is quite heavy.
   - The box is heavy enough to hold the door open.
7. (L2) Which word does “really” describe? The puppy is really small.
   - **small**
   - puppy
   - is
   - the
8. (L2) Which word tells to what degree? She barely made it to the bus.
   - **barely**
   - made
   - bus
   - She
9. (L3) Which sentence uses an adverb of degree to describe another adverb?
   - **He spoke quite slowly.**
   - He spoke slowly.
   - He is quite tall for his age.
   - He was slow.
10. (L3) Which sentence means the cookies are not finished baking?
   - **The cookies are nearly done.**
   - The cookies are completely done.
   - The cookies are totally done.
   - The cookies are done.
11. (L3) Which word is NOT an adverb of degree?
   - **quickly**
   - very
   - rather
   - extremely
12. (L3) Which sentence avoids a mistake in formal writing?
   - **Every snowflake is unique.**
   - Every snowflake is very unique.
   - Every snowflake is extremely unique.
   - Every snowflake is really very unique.
13. (L3) Which sentence uses “too” to mean “more than is good”?
   - **The bath water is too hot to get in.**
   - I want to come too.
   - She has two cats and a dog.
   - Can I have some ice cream and a cookie too?
14. (L3) Which sentence shows the smallest amount?
   - **I am slightly hungry.**
   - I am very hungry.
   - I am extremely hungry.
   - I am really hungry.

Newer types:

- Tap the words (L1): Tap the adverb of degree. The lake was **extremely** calm this morning.
- Sort (L2): Does each word make the meaning stronger or weaker? extremely → **Stronger**; slightly → **Weaker**; incredibly → **Stronger**; barely → **Weaker**; totally → **Stronger**; somewhat → **Weaker**
- Short answer (at least 15 words): Explain the difference between “The tea is very hot” and “The tea is too hot.”
  - Rubric: Says “very hot” means hot to a high degree / Says “too hot” means more than is wanted, so you can’t drink it yet
  - Model: “Very hot” just means it is really hot. “Too hot” means it is hotter than it should be, so you have to wait before you drink it.

## Judgment calls to check

- Articles count as adjectives in Khan’s lessons, so adjective questions say “skip a, an, and the” or don’t offer articles as choices.
- “I feel bad” (not “badly”) and “the better of the two” follow formal usage; the prompts say “in formal writing.”
- “Every snowflake is unique” (not “very unique”) follows the formal rule that “unique” doesn’t take “very.”
- Commas: “a heavy winter coat” and “a bright summer day” get no comma because the second word works with the noun as one idea.
