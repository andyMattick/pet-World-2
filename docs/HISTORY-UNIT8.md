- **Videos.**
  - Every Khan video in each lesson is its own ▶ button, 38 in all, with Khan's exact titles.
  - Each lesson link opens the start of the Khan lesson.
  - 8.7 has no videos, so its 🎬 Watch first prompt opens the Khan lesson instead (the same way as Unit 5's 5.3).
- **8.7 The Holocaust is a hard lesson** (`gate:true, quiet:true`, see `HARD-LESSONS.md`).
  - The first time she opens its practice, a grown-up previews it and unlocks it with the PIN.
  - Its practice saves progress, but there's no celebration and no arcade minutes.
  - It has no role card. She reads the sources as a historian.
  - The questions name victims as people (Anne Frank) and focus on the lesson's question, "How did the world respond?":
    - laws that took away rights
    - Kristallnacht
    - the Évian Conference and the St. Louis
    - rescuers like Chiune Sugihara and Manuel Quezon
    - the Allies' 1942 declaration
    - the Nuremberg trials
  - There is no graphic detail.
  - Its questions also appear in Khan's Quiz 3 and the unit test, which use the usual quiz screens.
- **Other hard topics** are written factually with no graphic detail:
  - the atomic bombings (8.6)
  - Pinochet (8.8)
  - the partition of India (8.9)
- **Roles.** She plays a witness or worker from the time:
  - a newspaper reporter in Sarajevo (1914)
  - a nurse at a field hospital (1917)
  - a reporter at the Paris Peace Conference (1919)
  - a radio reporter in 1930s Europe
  - a wartime factory worker (1943)
  - a journalist covering the Cold War
  - a student in Accra on Ghana's independence night (1957)
  - She never plays a perpetrator.
- **Questions.**
  - There are 117 multiple-choice questions, 14–15 per practice, at all three levels (43 at level 3).
  - Every practice has short adapted sources, including:
    - Edward Grey's "lamps" quote and the 1914 ultimatum
    - an Indian soldier's censored letter
    - Article 231 and the Balfour Declaration
    - a Mussolini paraphrase
    - Chamberlain, Roosevelt and the Einstein letter
    - the Nuremberg Laws and the 1942 Allied declaration
    - Churchill's "iron curtain" and Kennedy's 1962 speech
    - Nkrumah and Ho Chi Minh
  - No right answer is the longest choice, and `check-content` reports 0 warnings.
- **Newer-type questions.** Each practice has 2 scored newer-type questions (sort, select all, order, or Part A/B) and 1 short answer. The unit test ends with a paragraph on Hobsbawm's "Age of Extremes."
- **Projects (she picks one).**
  - 📁 Peace Museum Exhibit Card (`history8`): one turning point from 1914 to 1991, with its causes, the people affected, its evidence, and what changed.
  - Voices of the Century Interview (`history8lab`): she interviews an older relative about the Cold War, decolonization, or another part of the century, and connects it to the unit.
- **Test.** The unit test has 16 questions (2 per practice), and 12 right passes. The Khan unit test link is in `KHAN_UNIT_TESTS`.
