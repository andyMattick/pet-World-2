## End marks, the comma, and lists (End marks and commas Quiz)

### Three ways to end a sentence
_Lesson:_ Every sentence ends with one end mark. A statement (it tells something) and a command (it tells someone to do something) end with a period. A question ends with a question mark. A sentence that shows strong feeling, like surprise or alarm, ends with an exclamation point. A sentence that only reports a question, like “She asked if I was hungry,” is a statement, so it ends with a period.

   **?** · . · ! · ,

   **Our class is going to the zoo on Friday.** · Our class is going to the zoo on Friday? · Our class is going to the zoo on Friday, · Our class is going, to the zoo on Friday.
3. (level 1) Which end mark shows strong feeling?
   **An exclamation point** · A period at the very end · A question mark · A comma
4. (level 1) Which sentence is a question?
   **Have you ever seen a shooting star?** · I have never seen a shooting star. · What a bright shooting star that was! · Look at that shooting star over there.
5. (level 2) Which sentence should end with a question mark?

6. (level 2) What kind of sentence is this, and how should it end? Close the door, please
   **A command, so it ends with a period** · A question, so it ends with a question mark · A statement, so it ends with a question mark · An exclamation, so it ends with a comma
7. (level 2) Choose the end mark that shows the speaker is shouting with excitement: We won the championship___
   **!** · ? · , · :
8. (level 2) Which sentence uses its end mark correctly?
   **Watch out for that bike!** · What time does the bus leave! · Did you remember your lunch. · Is this seat taken.
9. (level 2) What kind of sentence ends with a period?
   **A statement that tells something** · A question that asks something · A sentence that asks for an answer · A sentence that shows great surprise
10. (level 3) Which sentence is punctuated correctly?

### Meet the comma
_Lesson:_ A comma marks a small pause inside a sentence and keeps words that belong apart from running together. Commas separate items in a list, come after an introduction, set off the name of someone you are talking to, and join two complete sentences when a joining word like and, but, or so comes after the comma. A comma never ends a sentence, and it never goes between a subject and its verb.

1. (level 1) What does a comma usually show?
   **A short pause inside a sentence** · The very end of a sentence · That a question is being asked of someone · That letters are missing
2. (level 1) Which sentence uses a comma correctly?
   **After lunch, we played tag.** · After lunch we, played tag. · After, lunch we played tag. · After lunch we played, tag.
3. (level 1) Which is a comma?
   **,** · . · ’ · ?
4. (level 2) Which sentence uses a comma to join two complete sentences correctly?
   **I wanted to swim, but the pool was closed.** · I wanted to swim but, the pool was closed. · I wanted, to swim but the pool was closed. · I wanted to swim but the pool, was closed.
5. (level 2) Where does the comma belong? Kai can you help me carry this box?
   **After “Kai”** · After “help” · After “carry” · After “this”
6. (level 2) Which sentence has a comma that should NOT be there?
   **My little brother, likes trains.** · My little brother likes trains, buses, and boats. · Yes, my little brother likes trains. · My little brother likes trains, and I like boats.
7. (level 2) Why does this sentence need a comma? “Let’s eat Grandma” should be “Let’s eat, Grandma.”
   **The comma shows we are talking to Grandma, not about eating her.** · The comma shows that the sentence is a question. · The comma shows that the word “eat” is a noun. · The comma shows that some letters are missing from the word Grandma.
8. (level 2) Which sentence uses a comma correctly?
   **Yes, I fed the fish.** · Yes I fed, the fish. · Yes I, fed the fish. · Yes I fed the fish,
9. (level 3) Which sentence is punctuated correctly?
   **The sky turned dark, so we went inside.** · The sky turned dark so, we went inside. · The sky, turned dark so we went inside. · The sky turned dark, so, we went inside.
10. (level 3) Which sentence shows a comma between a subject and its verb, which is a mistake?
   **The girl in the yellow raincoat, waved to us.** · The girl waved to us, and then we both waved back. · Waving to us, the girl smiled. · The girl, my cousin, waved to us.
11. (level 3) Which sentence needs a comma?
   **We can play outside or we can bake cookies.** · We can play outside or bake some cookies. · We played outside and then baked cookies together. · We played outside until dinner.
12. (level 3) Which sentence uses a comma in place of an end mark, which is a mistake?
   **The bus was late, we walked to school.** · The bus was late, so we walked to school. · Because the bus was late, we walked to school. · The bus was late; we walked to school.

   **One** · Two · Three · None

### Punctuating lists
_Lesson:_ Put a comma after each item in a list of three or more: We packed apples, cheese, and crackers. Pet World uses a comma before the last and (the serial or Oxford comma), because it can keep the meaning clear; some writers leave it out. A list of only two items gets no comma: apples and cheese. Adjectives that each describe the noun on their own get commas too: a long, cold, rainy day.

1. (level 1) Which sentence punctuates the list correctly?
   **We saw lions, zebras, and giraffes.** · We saw lions zebras and giraffes. · We saw, lions, zebras, and giraffes. · We saw lions, zebras and, giraffes.
2. (level 1) How many items are in this list? I need flour, sugar, eggs, and butter.
   **Four** · Three · Two · Five
3. (level 1) Which list of two items is punctuated correctly?
   **I ordered a sandwich and a salad.** · I ordered a sandwich, and a salad. · I ordered, a sandwich and a salad. · I ordered a sandwich and, a salad.
4. (level 2) Which sentence is punctuated correctly?
   **My favorite colors are red, orange, and purple.** · My favorite colors are red, orange, and, purple. · My favorite colors are, red, orange, and purple. · My favorite colors, are red orange and purple.
5. (level 2) Where are commas needed? Pack a toothbrush pajamas and a pillow.
   **After “toothbrush” and after “pajamas”** · After “Pack” and after “toothbrush” and “a” · After “pajamas” and after “and” · After “a” and after “pajamas”
6. (level 2) Which sentence punctuates a list of actions correctly?
   **She jumped, spun, and landed on her feet.** · She jumped spun, and landed, on her feet. · She jumped, spun and, landed on her feet. · She, jumped, spun, and landed on her feet.
7. (level 2) What is the comma before the last “and” in a list called?
   **The serial (Oxford) comma** · The ending comma · The question comma · The apostrophe-and-list comma
8. (level 2) Which sentence uses commas between describing words correctly?
   **It was a long, cold, rainy day.** · It was a long cold, rainy, day. · It was, a long, cold, rainy day. · It was a long, cold, rainy, day.
9. (level 3) Why can a comma before the last “and” help? I thank my parents, Ms. Lee, and Coach Ray.
   **It shows these are three separate people, not that Ms. Lee and Coach Ray are my parents.** · It shows that Coach Ray is the most important and most honored person in the whole list. · It shows that the sentence is really a question. · It shows that Ms. Lee and Coach Ray belong to my parents.
10. (level 3) Which sentence is punctuated correctly?
   **For lunch we had soup, grilled cheese, and peanut butter and jelly.** · For lunch we had soup, grilled cheese, and peanut butter, and jelly. · For lunch we had soup grilled, cheese, and peanut butter and jelly. · For lunch, we had, soup, grilled cheese, and peanut butter and jelly.
11. (level 3) Which sentence needs NO commas?
   **Leo and Ava painted the fence.** · Leo Ava and Max painted the fence. · Leo painted Ava sanded and Max cleaned up. · We bought nails screws and glue.
12. (level 3) Which sentence lists three places correctly?
   **This summer we visited Boston, New York, and Washington.** · This summer we visited Boston, New, York, and Washington. · This summer we visited, Boston, New York and, Washington. · This summer, we visited Boston New York and Washington.
13. (level 3) Which sentence is NOT punctuated correctly?
   **I like to read, draw and, swim.** · I like to read, draw, and swim. · I like to read and swim. · I like reading, drawing, and swimming.

## Commas in space and time (Commas in space and time Quiz)

### Salutations, valedictions, dates, and addresses
_Lesson:_ A friendly letter opens with a greeting (salutation) followed by a comma: Dear Grandpa, and closes with a sign-off (valediction) followed by a comma (Love, or Sincerely,). A date gets a comma between the day and the year (May 4, 2026), and another comma after the year when the sentence keeps going. A place gets a comma between the city and the state (Austin, Texas), and another after the state when the sentence keeps going. No comma goes between a month and a year alone (May 2026) or between a state and a ZIP code.

### Commas and introductory elements
_Lesson:_ When a sentence starts with an introduction before the main part, put a comma after the introduction. It can be a word (However, Finally, Yes), a phrase (After the long game, To get ready for the trip), or a clause, a part with its own subject and verb that can’t stand alone (When the bell rang, Because it rained). The comma shows where the introduction ends and the main sentence begins.

1. (level 1) Which sentence uses a comma after an introductory word?
   **Finally, the rain stopped.** · Finally the rain, stopped. · Finally the, rain stopped. · Finally the rain stopped,
2. (level 1) Where does the comma go? However the store was already closed.
   **After “However”** · After “store” · After “was” · After “already”
3. (level 1) Which sentence is punctuated correctly?
   **After the long game, we ate pizza.** · After the long game we, ate pizza. · After, the long game we ate pizza. · After the long, game we ate pizza.
4. (level 2) Which sentence is punctuated correctly?
   **When the bell rang, everyone ran outside.** · When the bell rang everyone, ran outside. · When, the bell rang everyone ran outside. · When the bell, rang everyone ran outside.

6. (level 2) What is the introductory part of this sentence? To get ready for the trip, Mom made a checklist.
   **To get ready for the trip** · Mom made a checklist for it · made a checklist · for the trip, Mom made a
7. (level 2) Which sentence is punctuated correctly?
   **Excited about the news, Lena called her best friend.** · Excited about the news Lena, called her best friend. · Excited, about the news Lena called her best friend. · Excited about the news, Lena called, her best friend.
8. (level 2) Which word at the start of a sentence needs a comma after it?
   **Meanwhile** · Dogs · Seven · Running
9. (level 3) Which sentence is punctuated correctly?

## More ways to use commas (More ways to use commas Quiz)

### Commas in dialogue, tag questions, direct address, and yes-or-no responses
_Lesson:_ In dialogue, a comma separates the speaker tag from the quotation: Mia said, “Let’s go.” or “Let’s go,” Mia said. (A question mark or exclamation point inside the quotation replaces the comma.) A tag question at the end gets a comma: You’re coming, aren’t you? Use commas to set off the name of someone you are talking to (Leo, come here. Thanks, Mom.) and words like yes, no, and well at the start of an answer (Yes, I finished.).

### Appositives
_Lesson:_ An appositive is a noun or noun phrase right next to another noun that renames it: My dog, a lazy beagle, sleeps all day. When the appositive just adds extra information, set it off with commas on both sides (or one comma if it ends the sentence). When it is needed to tell which one you mean, use no commas: my friend Ava (when you have more than one friend), the poet Langston Hughes.

1. (level 1) What is the appositive in this sentence? My dog, a lazy beagle, sleeps all day.
   **a lazy beagle** · My dog · sleeps in the sun · My dog sleeps
2. (level 1) What does an appositive do?
   **It renames a noun right next to it.** · It ends a sentence with strong feeling. · It joins two complete sentences. · It shows that something belongs to someone.
3. (level 1) Which sentence uses commas around the appositive correctly?
   **Ms. Patel, our music teacher, plays the cello.** · Ms. Patel our music teacher, plays the cello. · Ms. Patel, our music teacher plays the cello. · Ms. Patel our, music teacher, plays the cello.
4. (level 2) Which sentence is punctuated correctly?
   **We visited Yellowstone, a famous national park.** · We visited Yellowstone a famous national park. · We visited, Yellowstone, a famous national park. · We visited Yellowstone a famous, national park.
5. (level 2) Which noun does the appositive rename? The pizza, a giant pepperoni pie, disappeared fast.
   **pizza** · pie · pepperoni · fast
6. (level 2) Which sentence has an appositive?

7. (level 2) Which sentence is punctuated correctly?

8. (level 3) Which sentence correctly uses NO commas because the appositive tells which one?

9. (level 3) Kai has only one sister. Which sentence is punctuated correctly?
   **Kai’s sister, Nora, plays soccer.** · Kai’s sister Nora plays, soccer. · Kai’s sister Nora, plays on a soccer team. · Kai’s, sister Nora plays soccer.
10. (level 3) Which sentence is punctuated correctly?
   **I lent my book, a mystery about a lost ring, to Theo.** · I lent my book, a mystery about a lost ring to Theo. · I lent my book a mystery, about a lost ring, to Theo. · I lent my book a mystery about a lost ring, to Theo.
11. (level 3) Which sentence uses an appositive at the start?
   **A talented artist, Ruby painted the whole mural.** · Ruby painted the whole mural in less than a week. · Ruby, who loves art, painted the mural. · The mural that Ruby painted is huge.

## Apostrophes and contractions (Apostrophes and contractions Quiz)

### Meet the apostrophe
_Lesson:_ The apostrophe (’) has two main jobs. It stands in for missing letters in a contraction (do not → don’t), and it shows that something belongs to someone (the cat’s bowl). An apostrophe almost never makes a word plural: one dog, two dogs, with no apostrophe.
