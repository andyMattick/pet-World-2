## Introduction to colons (Colons Quiz)

### Introduction to colons
_Lesson:_ A colon (:) says “here comes what I promised.” Use it after a complete sentence to introduce a list, an example, or an explanation: I need three things: paper, glue, and tape. The words before the colon must be able to stand alone as a sentence, so a colon never comes right after a verb like are or include. Colons also separate numbers and parts: 3:45, a 2:1 ratio, a title and its subtitle, and the greeting of a business letter (Dear Ms. Ruiz:).

1. (level 1) Which sentence uses a colon correctly?
   **I packed three snacks: grapes, crackers, and cheese.** · I packed: grapes, crackers, and cheese for a snack. · I packed three: snacks, grapes, crackers, and cheese. · I packed three snacks, grapes: crackers, and cheese.
2. (level 1) What does a colon after a complete sentence tell the reader?
   **A list, an example, or an explanation comes next.** · The sentence is over, and a brand-new one begins. · Two words are being joined into one word. · Some of the letters in a word were left out.
3. (level 1) Which mark belongs in the blank? The movie starts at 4___15 p.m.
   **:** · ; · , · -
4. (level 2) Which sentence uses a colon correctly?
   **Dad gave me one job: walk the dog.** · Dad gave: me one job, walk the dog. · Dad gave me: one job, walk the dog. · Dad: gave me one job, walk the dog.
5. (level 2) Which sentence uses a colon incorrectly?
   **My favorite fruits are: mangoes and pears.** · I have two favorite fruits: mangoes and pears. · Here is my favorite snack: mango slices. · Remember the rule: wash the fruit first.
6. (level 2) Which greeting for a business letter is punctuated correctly?

7. (level 2) Which shows a book title and its subtitle written correctly?

   **The second part explains the first part.** · It sets apart a list of three or more things. · It separates the hour from the minutes. · It comes right after a verb like is.
9. (level 3) Which sentence uses a colon incorrectly?

10. (level 3) Which sentence is punctuated correctly?

## Introduction to semicolons (Semicolons Quiz)

### Introduction to semicolons
_Lesson:_ A semicolon (;) joins two complete sentences that are closely related, with no and, but, or so between them: The rain stopped; the sun came out. Both sides of a semicolon must be able to stand alone as sentences. A semicolon also comes before a joining word like however or therefore, with a comma after that word (I studied; however, the test was hard), and it separates the items in a list when the items already have commas inside them.

1. (level 1) Which sentence uses a semicolon correctly?
   **The bus was late; we walked to school.** · The bus; was late, we walked to school. · The bus was late we; walked to school. · Because the bus was late; we walked.
2. (level 1) What can a semicolon join?
   **Two complete sentences that are closely related** · A sentence and a word group that can’t stand alone · A greeting and the name of the person · Two words that make one describing word
3. (level 1) Which mark is a semicolon?
   **;** · : · , · —
4. (level 2) Which sentence is punctuated correctly?
   **Maya loves soccer; her brother prefers chess.** · Maya loves soccer; her brother, prefers chess. · Maya loves; soccer her brother prefers chess. · Maya loves soccer her brother; prefers chess.
5. (level 2) Which sentence uses a semicolon incorrectly?
   **After the movie ended; we went home.** · The movie ended; we went home. · The movie ended late; we got home at ten. · The movie was long; however, it was fun.
6. (level 2) Which sentence correctly uses a semicolon with “however”?
   **I studied hard; however, the test was tricky.** · I studied hard, however; the test was tricky. · I studied hard; however the test, was tricky. · I studied; hard however, the test was tricky.
7. (level 2) Why are semicolons used in this sentence? We visited Austin, Texas; Tulsa, Oklahoma; and Denver, Colorado.
   **The list items already have commas inside them.** · Each city is a complete sentence by itself. · A semicolon comes before each place name. · The list explains the first part of the sentence.
8. (level 2) Which pair of sentences could be joined with a semicolon?
   **The lights went out. Everyone grabbed a flashlight.** · The lights went out. Because of the storm. · When the lights in the gym went out. Everyone grabbed a flashlight. · The lights. Went out during the storm.
9. (level 3) Which sentence is punctuated correctly?
   **The team practiced daily; as a result, they won the title.** · The team practiced daily, as a result; they won the title. · The team practiced daily; as a result they won, the title. · The team practiced; daily as a result, they won the title.
10. (level 3) Which list is punctuated correctly?
   **The winners were Ava, age 10; Ben, age 11; and Cy, age 12.** · The winners were Ava; age 10, Ben; age 11, and Cy; age 12. · The winners were: Ava, age 10; Ben, age 11; and Cy, age 12. · The winners were Ava, age 10; Ben, age 11; and; Cy, age 12.
11. (level 3) Which sentence needs a semicolon to be correct?
   **My cat sleeps by day she plays at night.** · My cat sleeps by day, and she plays at night. · My cat sleeps by day but plays at night. · My cat sleeps by day and plays at night.
12. (level 3) Where should the semicolon go? The library opens at nine the doors are locked until then.
   **After “nine”** · After “library” · After “opens” · After “doors”
13. (level 3) Which sentence uses a semicolon correctly?
   **Leo forgot his lunch; luckily, Sam shared.** · Leo forgot; his lunch luckily, Sam shared. · Leo forgot his lunch; luckily Sam, shared. · Since Leo forgot his lunch; Sam shared.
14. (level 3) What must be true of the words on each side of a semicolon that joins two clauses?
   **Each side could stand alone as a sentence.** · The second side starts with a capital letter. · The first side has to be a list of things. · The second side begins with and or but.

### Using semicolons and commas
_Lesson:_ There are two ways to join two complete sentences: a comma plus a joining word (and, but, or, so, yet, for, nor), or a semicolon with no joining word. A comma alone can’t hold two sentences together; that mistake is called a comma splice. If one part can’t stand alone, like “When the bell rang,” use a comma, not a semicolon.

1. (level 1) Which mark belongs in the blank? I wanted pizza___ my sister wanted tacos.
   **;** · , · : · -
2. (level 1) Which mark belongs in the blank? I wanted pizza___ but my sister wanted tacos.
   **,** · ; · : · —
3. (level 1) What mistake does this sentence make? We ran to the bus, it had already left.
   **It joins two complete sentences with a comma alone.** · It needs a colon before the word “it.” · It needs an apostrophe in the word “bus.” · It needs a colon after “bus” because a list comes next.
4. (level 2) Which sentence is punctuated correctly?
   **The puppy was tired, so it took a nap.** · The puppy was tired, it took a nap. · The puppy was tired so, it took a nap. · The puppy, was tired so it took a nap.
5. (level 2) Which sentence is a comma splice?
   **The game ended, everyone cheered.** · The game ended; everyone cheered. · The game ended, and everyone cheered. · When the game ended, everyone cheered.
6. (level 2) How can you fix this sentence? The oven is hot, be careful.
   **Change the comma to a semicolon.** · Add a comma after the word “be.” · Take out the comma and add nothing. · Add a semicolon after the word “be.”
7. (level 2) Which mark belongs in the blank? Although it was raining___ we played outside.
   **,** · ; · : · —
8. (level 2) Which sentence uses a semicolon correctly?
   **Ty finished his chores; then he called Ana.** · Ty finished his chores; and called Ana. · Ty finished; his chores then he called Ana. · After Ty finished his chores; he called Ana.
9. (level 3) Which sentence is punctuated correctly?
   **Ella can sing, and she can play the drums.** · Ella can sing, she can play the drums. · Ella can sing and, she can play the drums. · Ella can sing; she can, play the drums.
10. (level 3) Which sentence needs a comma, not a semicolon, in the blank?
   **Before the race started___ we stretched.** · The race started___ we ran fast. · The race was long___ my legs were sore. · I trained for weeks___ the race felt easy.
11. (level 3) Which joins these two sentences correctly? The storm knocked out the power. We played board games by candlelight.
   **The storm knocked out the power; we played board games by candlelight.** · The storm knocked out the power, we played board games by candlelight. · The storm knocked out the power; so we played board games, by candlelight. · The storm knocked out; the power we played board games by candlelight.
12. (level 3) Which sentence is punctuated correctly?
   **My uncle lives in Ohio; my aunt lives in Maine.** · My uncle lives in Ohio, my aunt lives in Maine. · My uncle lives in Ohio; but, my aunt lives in Maine. · My uncle lives in Ohio my aunt, lives in Maine.
13. (level 3) Which mark belongs in the blank? We could take the train___ or we could ride our bikes.
   **,** · ; · : · .
14. (level 3) Which correctly fixes the comma splice? Marcus studied for hours, he still felt nervous.
   **Marcus studied for hours; he still felt nervous.** · Marcus studied for hours; but, he still felt nervous. · Marcus studied for hours he still felt, nervous. · Marcus, studied for hours, he still felt nervous.

### Using semicolons and colons
_Lesson:_ Both marks come after a complete sentence, but they do different jobs. A colon points ahead: what comes after it explains, names, or lists what the first part set up (I know what to bring: snacks). A semicolon balances two separate, related sentences of equal weight (I brought snacks; Ava brought drinks). Ask: Does the second part explain or list? Use a colon. Is it a second idea that could stand on its own? Use a semicolon.

   **:** · ; · -
2. (level 1) Which mark belongs in the blank? Jada likes cats___ her sister likes dogs.
   **;** · : · ,

8. (level 2) When should you choose a colon instead of a semicolon?

   **:** · ; · -

## Formatting styles (Formatting styles Quiz)

### Italics, underlines, and quotation marks
_Lesson:_ Titles of long, whole works go in italics when you type and are underlined when you write by hand: books, movies, TV series, albums, magazines, newspapers, plays, and video games. Titles of short works, or of parts of a bigger work, go in quotation marks: songs, poems, short stories, chapters, articles, and single TV episodes. Ask: Is it a whole work, or a part of one?

### Introduction to parentheses
_Lesson:_ Parentheses ( ) hold extra information the sentence could do without, like a side note: My cousin (the one who sings) is visiting. Read the sentence without the words in parentheses; it should still make sense. A comma or end mark that belongs to the main sentence goes after the closing parenthesis, but if a whole sentence is inside the parentheses, its end mark goes inside too. Parentheses also hold abbreviations, like the National Park Service (NPS), and numbers in a list.

## Hyphens, dashes, and ellipses (Hyphens, dashes, and ellipses Quiz)

### Introduction to dashes
_Lesson:_ A dash (—) is a long line that makes the reader stop for something important or surprising. A pair of dashes sets off an interruption or extra detail in the middle of a sentence, more strongly than commas or parentheses: My sister—who hates cooking—made dinner. A single dash can set up a dramatic ending or a sudden change of thought: I opened the box and found—a frog! A dash is longer than a hyphen, and it doesn’t join words together.

1. (level 1) Which mark is a dash?
   **—** · - · _ · ~
2. (level 1) What does a dash do in a sentence?
   **It makes the reader pause for something important or surprising.** · It joins two short words together to make one describing word, like well-known. · It shows that letters are missing from a word. · It ends a sentence that asks a question.
3. (level 1) Which sentence uses a dash correctly?
   **I opened the door and saw—a giant cake!** · I opened—the door and saw a giant cake! · I—opened the door and saw a giant cake! · I opened the door and saw a giant—cake!
4. (level 2) Which sentence uses a pair of dashes correctly?
   **My uncle—a real pilot—gave us a tour.** · My uncle—a real pilot gave us a tour—. · My uncle a real—pilot gave—us a tour. · My—uncle a real pilot—gave us a tour.
5. (level 2) Which marks could replace the commas and make the extra detail stand out more? Our coach, who is usually calm, jumped up and yelled.
   **A pair of dashes** · A pair of quotation marks · A pair of apostrophes · A pair of semicolons
6. (level 2) Why is a dash used here? We searched the whole house for the keys and found them—in the fridge.
   **To set up a surprising ending** · To join two words into one word · To show that letters are missing · To introduce a long list of items
7. (level 2) Which sentence is punctuated correctly?
   **There’s one thing I want for my birthday—a puppy.** · There’s one—thing I want for my birthday a puppy. · There’s one thing I want—for my birthday a puppy. · There’s—one thing I want for my birthday a puppy.
8. (level 2) Which other mark could replace the dash without changing the meaning much? I know what we need—more snacks.
   **A colon** · A semicolon · A hyphen · An apostrophe
9. (level 3) Which sentence uses dashes correctly?
   **The test—all forty questions of it—took an hour.** · The test—all forty questions of it, took an hour. · The test, all forty questions of it—took an hour. · The test—all forty questions—of it took an hour.
10. (level 3) Which sentence uses a dash to show a sudden break in thought?
   **I was going to call you—wait, is that my phone ringing?** · We need three things: paper, glue, and scissors. · My cousin (the one who lives in Utah) is visiting next week. · The well-known chef opened a new café downtown.
11. (level 3) Why might a writer use dashes instead of commas here? The winner—to everyone’s shock—was the youngest player.
   **Dashes make the interruption stand out more.** · Commas can’t set off extra information. · Dashes show that the sentence is a question. · Dashes join the words into one word.
12. (level 3) Which sentence is punctuated correctly?
   **Three friends—Ana, Bo, and Cleo—came to the party.** · Three friends, Ana, Bo, and Cleo—came to the party. · Three friends—Ana, Bo, and Cleo, came to the party. · Three friends—Ana, Bo—and Cleo came to the party.
13. (level 3) Where should a dash go to set up the surprise? After hours of digging, the pirates found the treasure chest empty.
   **Before “empty”** · Before “hours” · Before “the pirates” · Before “digging”
14. (level 3) Which sentence uses a dash incorrectly?
   **She is a well—known author.** · She is a famous author—at least in our town. · She—a famous author—visited our school. · She wrote one book—and it became a hit.

### Dashes and hyphens
_Lesson:_ A hyphen (-) is short and joins words or word parts into one: a well-known song, forty-two, my ex-coach, self-control. Hyphenate two or more words that work together to describe a noun when they come before it (a ten-year-old dog), but not when they come after it (the dog is ten years old), and not after a word ending in -ly (a brightly lit room). A dash (—) is long and separates parts of a sentence to show a pause, an interruption, or a surprise.

1. (level 1) Which mark is a hyphen?
   **-** · — · : · ;
2. (level 1) What does a hyphen do?
   **It joins words or word parts into one.** · It sets off a surprising ending. · It shows a pause or an interruption. · It introduces a list after a sentence.
3. (level 1) Which number is written correctly?
   **forty-six** · forty six · forty—six · fortysix
4. (level 2) What is the mark doing in this sentence? Our old dog—who still loves to fetch—ran across the yard.
   **It’s a dash that separates an extra detail.** · It’s a hyphen that joins two words into one. · It’s a hyphen that shows letters are missing. · It’s a dash that joins two describing words.
5. (level 2) What is the mark doing in this sentence? We watched a well-made movie.
   **It’s a hyphen that joins two words into one.** · It’s a dash that sets off an interruption. · It’s a dash that shows a sudden surprise. · It’s a hyphen that splits a sentence in two.
6. (level 2) Which sentence is punctuated correctly?
   **We adopted a three-year-old cat.** · We adopted a three year-old cat. · We adopted a three—year—old cat. · We adopted a three-year old cat.
7. (level 2) Which sentence is punctuated correctly?
   **The puppy is three months old.** · The puppy is three-months-old. · The puppy is three—months—old. · The puppy is three months-old.
8. (level 2) Which word is written correctly?
   **self-confident** · self confident · self—confident · selfconfident
9. (level 3) Which sentence uses a hyphen and a dash correctly?
   **My best friend—a world-famous juggler—is visiting.** · My best friend-a world—famous juggler-is visiting. · My best—friend a world-famous juggler—is visiting. · My best friend—a world famous—juggler is visiting.
10. (level 3) Which sentence is punctuated correctly?
   **The brightly lit room was full of balloons.** · The brightly-lit room was full of balloons. · The brightly—lit room was full of balloons. · The bright-ly lit room was full of balloons.
11. (level 3) Which sentence is punctuated correctly?
   **She wrote a two-page letter.** · She wrote a two page-letter. · She wrote a two—page letter. · She wrote a two page letter.
12. (level 3) Which sentence uses hyphens correctly?
   **My great-grandmother is ninety-one.** · My great grand-mother is ninety one. · My great-grand-mother is ninety-one. · My great grandmother is ninety-one.
13. (level 3) Why does “chocolate-covered” have a hyphen in “a chocolate-covered pretzel”?
   **The two words work together to describe the pretzel.** · The second word is a surprise, so a hyphen sets it apart. · A hyphen goes between any two long words. · It shows that some letters were left out.
14. (level 3) Which sentence is punctuated correctly?
   **We got home at midnight—tired, cold, and happy.** · We got home at midnight-tired, cold, and happy. · We got home at mid-night—tired, cold, and happy. · We got home at midnight—tired, cold—and happy.

  Model: We have a two-year-old hamster. Our hamster is two years old. The hyphens join the words into one describing word before the noun, but after the noun they are just regular words.

### Introduction to ellipses
_Lesson:_ An ellipsis is three dots (…) that show something was left out or that a voice trails off. When you quote someone and skip some of their words, put an ellipsis where the words were, and make sure the shorter quotation still means what the speaker meant. In dialogue and stories, an ellipsis can also show a pause, hesitation, or suspense: “Well… I guess so.”

1. (level 1) Which mark is an ellipsis?
   **…** · : · — · ;
2. (level 1) When you shorten a quotation, what does an ellipsis show?
   **Where words were left out** · Where the speaker got angry · Where a new speaker starts · Where the title of the book is
3. (level 1) How many dots make an ellipsis?
   **Three** · Two · Four · Five
4. (level 2) Original: “Our town library, which opened in 1920, lends books, games, and even fishing poles.” Which shortened quotation uses the ellipsis correctly?
   **“Our town library … lends books, games, and even fishing poles.”** · “Our town library, which opened … books.” · “Our town library … opened in 1920 games.” · “Our town library, which opened … in 1920, lends books and games.”
5. (level 2) Why might a writer use an ellipsis in a quotation?
   **To leave out words that aren’t needed** · To add some words that the speaker didn’t say · To show the speaker was shouting · To change what the speaker meant
6. (level 2) What does the ellipsis show? “I… I didn’t mean to break it,” Tommy whispered.
   **Tommy is nervous and stops for a moment.** · Some of Tommy’s words were left out of a quote. · Tommy is reading a title out loud. · Tommy is listing several things.
7. (level 2) Which sentence shows someone pausing to think?
   **Maybe we could… no, let’s stay home.** · Maybe we could: no, let’s stay home. · Maybe we could; no, let’s stay home. · Maybe we could (no), let’s stay home.
8. (level 2) Original: “The storm knocked down trees, flooded three streets, and closed the school for two days.” Which shortened quotation is correct?
   **“The storm knocked down trees … and closed the school for two days.”** · “The storm … closed three streets.” · “The storm knocked down … the school for two days.” · “The storm knocked down trees, flooded three … the school for two days.”
9. (level 3) Coach Lee said, “Practice does not make you perfect, but it does make you better.” Which shortened version changes what the coach meant?
   **“Practice does … make you perfect.”** · “Practice … does make you better.” · “Practice does not make you perfect, but … make you better.” · “Practice does not make you perfect … but it does make you better.”
10. (level 3) Original: “The zoo, which is open every day, has over two hundred animals.” Ana wants to quote only the part about the animals. Which is correct?
   **“The zoo … has over two hundred animals.”** · “The zoo, which is open every day … animals.” · “The zoo has over two hundred animals.” · “The zoo … is open … animals.”
11. (level 3) In which sentence does the ellipsis show that words were left out, not a pause in speech?
   **The sign read, “Visitors must … stay on the trail.”** · “I was going to tell you, but… never mind,” Jo said. · Um… can I have another cookie? · “Wait… is that thunder?” Max asked.
12. (level 3) Why should you check a shortened quotation against the original?
   **To make sure the ellipsis didn’t change what the speaker meant** · To make sure the quote has at least two ellipses · To make sure each sentence ends with an ellipsis · To make sure the ellipsis is placed at the very end of each sentence
13. (level 3) What does the ellipsis show? I opened the fridge, reached for the cake, and… it was gone.
   **A pause that builds suspense** · Words left out of a quotation · A list of items in the fridge · Two words joined into one
14. (level 3) Original: “Pandas spend most of the day eating, and they can eat about 40 pounds of bamboo.” Which shortened quotation is correct?
   **“Pandas … can eat about 40 pounds of bamboo.”** · “Pandas spend … about 40 pounds of bamboo.” · “Pandas can eat about 40 pounds of bamboo.” · “Pandas spend most of the day … 40 pounds of bamboo eating.”
