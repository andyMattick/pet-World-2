## Chromosomes


### Understand: Chromosomes (`chromU`)

*Lesson:* Chromosomes are long, coiled strands of DNA in the nucleus. A gene is a section of DNA with the instructions for a protein, and proteins build the body and do the cell’s work. Body cells have chromosomes in pairs, one of each pair from each parent.

   **A long, tightly coiled strand of DNA** · A chain of sugar molecules · A bundle of thin, stretchy fibers · A small bubble of water and salt
3. [1] A visitor asks what a gene is. What do you say?  
   **A section of DNA with instructions for a protein** · A whole chromosome made of many cells · A protein that carries oxygen in the blood · The part of a cell that makes sugar
4. [1] A grandparent asks how many copies of each chromosome a cow’s body cells have. What do you say?

### Apply: Chromosomes (`chromA`)

*Lesson:* Use chromosome counts and pairs to explain inheritance. A sex cell (egg or sperm) gets one chromosome from each pair, so it has half the body-cell number; when egg and sperm join, the pairs are whole again. The two chromosomes of a pair carry the same genes but can carry different alleles.

## Mutations (then Quiz 1)


### Apply: Mutations (`mutA`)

*Lesson:* A mutation is a change in the DNA of a gene. It can change the protein the gene makes, which can change a trait in a harmful, helpful, or neutral way. A cell reads DNA three letters at a time, so adding or removing one letter shifts every group after it. Only mutations in egg or sperm cells can be passed to offspring.

**Multiple choice** (level in brackets)

1. [1] A visitor asks what a mutation is. What do you say?  
   **A change in the DNA of a gene** · A brand-new chromosome that appears from nowhere · An injury, like a broken leg or a cut · A trait an animal learns while growing
2. [1] A kid asks if mutations are harmful. What do you say?  
   **They can be harmful, helpful, or have no effect** · Yes, mutations make animals sick · No, mutations make animals stronger · Yes, because a changed protein can’t work
3. [1] A visitor asks what can cause a mutation. What do you say?  
   **A mistake when DNA is copied, or things like UV light** · Eating food that has a lot of protein in it · Exercising the muscles very hard · Living in a barn with many other animals
4. [1] A mutation happens in one of a cow’s skin cells. A kid asks if her calves can inherit it. What do you say?  
   **No, only mutations in egg or sperm cells pass on** · Yes, mutations in any cell pass to the calves · Yes, but just to her female calves · No, mutations in cows fade before calving
5. [2] Belgian Blue cattle have a mutation that breaks the gene for myostatin, a protein that limits muscle growth. What trait do they show?  
   **Extra-large muscles** · Very small muscles · No horns · A roan coat
6. [2] A mutation changes one DNA letter, but the protein comes out the same. How can that be?  
   **The new three-letter code stands for the same amino acid** · The cell fixes the protein after it is made by checking it · Proteins are not made from the instructions in genes · One letter is too small for the cell to read
7. [2] A mutation was in the egg that became a calf. A visitor asks why the mutation is in nearly every cell of the calf. What do you explain?  
   **All its cells came from copies of that first cell** · The mutation spread through the blood like a cold · The calf caught it from its mother’s milk · Mutations copy themselves into new cells over the years
8. [2] Which is an example of a helpful mutation for a farm animal?  
   **A mutation that helps cattle resist a disease** · A mutation that makes calves too weak to stand · A mutation that stops a hen from making strong eggshells · A mutation that changes a DNA letter but leaves the protein the same
9. [3] A gene reads ATG GCA TTC AAA. A mutation adds one letter right after ATG. Why does this usually change more than one amino acid?  
   **Every three-letter group after it shifts** · The added letter becomes a new chromosome · One extra letter doubles the protein’s size · The cell reads the gene backward after a mutation
10. [3] Polled (hornless) Holsteins carry a natural mutation. A dairy farmer chooses polled bulls. What is one benefit?  
   **Calves won’t need their horns removed** · Calves will grow twice as fast · The cows will produce milk with more protein · The bulls can’t pass on any other mutations
11. [3] A mutation changes the shape of a protein. Which outcome could happen?

14. [3] Which change is NOT a mutation?  
   **A cow’s muscles getting bigger from exercise** · A DNA letter swapped in one of a bull’s sperm cells · A DNA letter lost while a cell copies its DNA · UV light changing a DNA letter in a cow’s skin cell

## Reproduction and genetic variation (then Quiz 2)


### Understand: Sexual reproduction and genetic variation (`sexVarU`)

*Lesson:* In sexual reproduction, an egg and a sperm from two parents join, so each offspring gets a new mix of alleles: brothers and sisters differ. In asexual reproduction one parent makes offspring with copies of its own genes. Variation helps some of a group survive changes like a new disease.

**Multiple choice** (level in brackets)

   **Two** · One · Three · It depends on the season
2. [1] A kid asks how the offspring’s genes compare to the parent’s in asexual reproduction. What do you say?  
   **They are copies of the parent’s genes** · Half are from each of two parents · They are a new mix each time · They come from many different parents
3. [1] Which two cells join in sexual reproduction?  
   **An egg cell and a sperm cell** · Two body cells · Two egg cells from one hen · A sperm cell and a skin cell
4. [1] A strawberry plant by the barn sends out a runner that grows into a new plant. What kind of reproduction is this?  
   **Asexual** · Sexual · Pollination · Fertilization
5. [2] A visitor asks why the piglets in one litter look different from each other. What do you explain?  
   **Each got a different mix of genes from the two parents** · The biggest piglet took the best genes first · Piglets copy the look of the piglet next to them · They each got different food first
6. [2] What is one advantage of sexual reproduction for a herd?  
   **Variety makes it likelier that some survive a new disease** · It needs just one parent to make offspring · Some offspring are exact copies of a strong parent · It is faster and makes more offspring
7. [2] What is one advantage of asexual reproduction?  
   **One parent can make many offspring quickly** · Offspring are more varied · Offspring can survive any new disease · It needs a male and a female
8. [2] Dolly the sheep (born 1996) was cloned from a body cell of a ewe. How did the genes in Dolly’s nucleus compare with that ewe’s?

### Apply: Genetics vocabulary (`vocabA`)

*Lesson:* An allele is one version of a gene. The genotype is the pair of alleles (like Pp); the phenotype is the trait you see (no horns). Homozygous means two of the same allele, heterozygous two different. A dominant allele shows with one copy; a recessive one shows only with two. In codominance both show (roan); in incomplete dominance they blend (blue).

## Unit test paragraph

You are the head tour guide at the Pet World Farm Barn. A polled (no horns) cow and a polled bull have had three calves together, and one of them has horns. A family asks how that happened and whether it could happen again. Write a paragraph that answers them using chromosomes, alleles, and a Punnett square, and explains one way sexual reproduction or mutations make calves different from their parents.  
Rubric: Explains that genes and alleles are on chromosomes and each calf gets one of each pair from each parent; Says the horned calf is pp and that its polled parents must carry a hidden p (Pp); Uses a Punnett square or probability correctly (1/4, or 25%, for a Pp × Pp cross); Explains how sexual reproduction or mutations create variation  
Model: *Each calf gets one chromosome of every pair from its mother and one from its father, so it gets one allele of the horn gene from each. The polled allele (P) is dominant and the horned allele (p) is recessive, so a calf with horns must be pp. That means its mother and the bull both carried a hidden p and are Pp. In a Pp × Pp Punnett square the boxes are PP, Pp, Pp and pp, so each new calf has a 1/4 (25%) chance of horns, and it could happen again. Because each egg and sperm carries a different mix of alleles, sexual reproduction makes brothers and sisters different, and a rare mutation in an egg or sperm could even add a brand-new allele.*
