## Why Do Empires Collapse? (5.2)  (15 multiple choice + 3 newer-type)

1. L1: A neighbor asks what historians call causes of collapse that come from inside an empire, like corruption or civil war. What do you say?
   - **Internal causes**
   - External causes
   - Natural causes
   - Foreign causes from beyond the borders
2. L1: Which is an external cause of an empire’s collapse?
   - **Invasions by groups from beyond the borders**
   - Officials taking bribes
   - Rival generals fighting each other for the emperor’s throne
   - Rising taxes on farmers
3. L2: Looking back: the Han dynasty fell in 220 CE. Which problems helped bring it down?
   - **Weak emperors, power struggles at court, and the Yellow Turban Rebellion**
   - An invasion by Roman legions that marched across Central Asia from the west
   - A war with Alexander’s generals
   - The arrival of Islam in China
4. L2: Germanic groups like the Goths settle inside the empire and later fight Rome. What kind of cause is this?
   - **External pressure on the borders**
   - A disease
   - Internal corruption
   - A change in the empire’s official religion
5. L1: In 476 CE, a Germanic general removed the last emperor in the West. Who was that emperor?
   - **Romulus Augustulus**
   - Augustus
   - Julius Caesar
   - Constantine the Great
6. L2: A traveler asks why the Roman Empire in the East is still standing. What do you say?
   - **It is richer and easier to defend, with Constantinople’s walls**
   - The eastern provinces have no borders that enemies can reach by land
   - Its emperors moved the army to Britain
   - The Goths promised to stay in the West
7. L3: A Roman priest named Salvian wrote (about 440 CE, adapted): “Poor farmers flee to the Goths, because they cannot bear Rome’s tax collectors.” What cause of collapse does this point to?
   - **Heavy taxes turned people against the empire**
   - Disease killed most of the farmers
   - The Goths refused to let anyone settle with them
   - Farmers wanted to move to Constantinople
8. L3: Salvian was a Christian priest who blamed Rome’s troubles on Romans’ own sins. How might this shape his account?
   - **He may stress Roman wrongdoing to make a moral point**
   - He may praise the tax collectors to please the emperor
   - He could not have known anything about taxes
   - He wrote it to describe life in Han China
9. L3: Historians say Rome and the Han both fell from many causes, not one. Which pair best shows this?
   - **Rebellions and weak rulers inside; invasions and raids from outside**
   - Plague in Rome, and floods in Han China, with no other troubles
   - Rome fell when Persia conquered it; the Han fell when Rome conquered it
   - Both fell when Islam spread into their lands
10. L2: As Rome weakens, many city families like yours move to country estates for safety. What is changing?
   - **Trade and town life are shrinking in much of the West**
   - Cities are growing larger than ever before
   - Farmers have stopped growing food
   - The Senate is moving from Rome to Gaul to be safer from the Goths
11. L2: A traveler asks what the Huns did to the peoples north of Rome. What do you say?
   - **Their attacks pushed groups like the Goths into Roman lands**
   - They became Roman citizens, and their leaders sat in the Senate
   - They built roads and forts for the Roman army
   - They traded silk with the Goths
12. L3: Which evidence best supports the claim that civil wars weakened Rome?
   - **In the 200s CE, dozens of emperors ruled, and most were killed**
   - Roman roads still reached Britain
   - Christianity spread to many cities and towns across the empire
   - Rome traded with Han China for silk
13. L2: Looking back: after 476, the Western Empire was gone, but Latin, Roman law, and the Church lived on. What does this show?
   - **Some things continued even after the empire collapsed**
   - Nothing Roman survived the collapse in the West
   - The Western Empire was rebuilt right away by a new emperor
   - Latin was replaced by Greek across the West
14. L1: Which city was the capital of the Roman Empire in the East?
   - **Constantinople**
   - Ravenna
   - Carthage
   - Antioch
15. L2: Looking back: what does “restructuring” mean after an empire collapses?
   - **People build new kinds of governments and ways of life**
   - Everyone goes back to hunting and gathering
   - The old empire is rebuilt exactly as it was, with the same rulers
   - People stop farming and trading

- Sort: Is each cause of collapse internal (inside the empire) or external (from outside)? Officials taking bribes → **Internal**; Huns and Goths crossing the border → **External**; Generals fighting each other for the throne → **Internal**; Raids by nomads on the frontier → **External**

From outside, groups like the Goths and Vandals crossed the borders and took over western provinces.*
